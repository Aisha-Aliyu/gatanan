Long ago, in a season when the rains forgot the land, Gizo the spider grew thin with hunger. He heard that the old woman at the edge of the village kept a clay pot that was never empty, so he went to her and begged for work.
The old woman said, "Tend my farm until the moon is full, and each evening you may eat from the pot. But take only what fills your hand, no more."
For many days Gizo obeyed, and each evening his hand was full and his belly content. But greed crept into him like a thief in the dark. One night he thought, "Why fill only my hand when I could fill a sack?" So he crept to the pot and reached in with both arms.
The pot closed upon his arms and would not let go until sunrise, when the whole village found him trapped and laughing at his own foolishness. From that day, they say, the spider hides in the corners of houses, ashamed to be seen reaching for more than he needs.
